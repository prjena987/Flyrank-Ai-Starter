Ranking Signal Analysis: CTR Opportunity Detection in Search Performance Data

Which content signals distinguish high-CTR from low-CTR pages at similar search positions?

FlyRank ML Internship Capstone Ranking Signal Analysis Lane Machine Learning Track

Dataset: FlyRank Internship Warehouse (starter slice, 30,000 rows)
Validation: Client-holdout split (8 test clients, 2,754 pages)
Code: github.com/prjena987/Flyrank-Ai-Starter

Abstract

Content teams face a prioritization problem: thousands of pages rank in search, but limited review capacity means only a subset can be optimized. We ask which observable content and engagement signals distinguish high-click-through-rate (CTR) pages from low-CTR pages when they occupy similar search positions. Using the FlyRank starter dataset of 30,000 content items across 32 pseudonymized clients, we define a position-tier-stratified label (top 25% vs. bottom 25% CTR within each tier) and compare a transparent rule-based baseline against logistic regression, decision tree, and random forest models under client-holdout validation. The random forest achieves ROC AUC 0.998 and Precision@50 1.000 on this synthetic slice, substantially outperforming the baseline. The strongest differentiating signals are engagement rate, content age, and scroll depth. We translate these findings into a ranked action playbook with five reason-coded recommendation classes. All results are observational and decision-support only; they do not establish causal impact of refreshes on recovery.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Search engine optimization at scale is a ranking problem in two senses: pages must rank on Google, and content teams must rank their own review queues. The second problem—which page should a human reviewer open first?—is the focus of this work.

A common pitfall in CTR analysis is comparing click-through rates across different positions. A page at position 2 naturally earns more clicks than a page at position 9, so raw CTR comparisons confound position quality with content quality. We solve this by stratifying all comparisons within position tiers (top 3, page 1, striking distance, pages 3–5, deep). Within each tier, we ask: given similar visibility, which pages over-capture or under-capture clicks?

The decision this work supports: Given a team capacity of 50 pages to review per week, which 50 should they prioritize for title/meta review, content expansion, or engagement improvement?

2. Data

2.1 Source & Scope

We use the FlyRank ML Internship starter dataset, a public-safe pseudonymized release of content_refresh_anonymized.csv (30,000 rows × 44 columns, 32 clients). All metrics aggregate a trailing 90-day window. The dataset contains observable signals only—no product decision flags, no raw queries, no client names or domains.

Note: Missingness is systematic by content_type. feedly article rows have no keyword data. We fill these with "unknown" for categoricals and 0 for numerics, acknowledging that zero encodes "no keyword context" for feedly articles.

3. Methodology

3.1 Label Definition

Within each position_tier, we compute the 25th and 75th percentiles of ctr. Pages at or above the 75th percentile are labeled high CTR (1); pages at or below the 25th percentile are labeled low CTR (0). The middle 50% are excluded to sharpen the classification signal.

3.2 Features

Numeric (20 features): search_volume, competition, cpc, word_count, char_count, content_age_days, days_since_last_update, log-transformed 90-day totals, days_with_impressions, days_with_sessions, engagement_rate, scroll_rate, ai_traffic_pct, plus binary flags.

Categorical (8 features): competition_level, content_type, main_intent, age_tier, freshness_tier, word_count_tier, impression_tier, position_tier.

3.3 Baseline

Before any machine learning, we construct a transparent rule-based score with six weighted signals:

  • High engagement rate (>50%): +0.25
  • High scroll rate (>60%): +0.15
  • Fresh content (≤90 days since update): +0.20
  • Deep content (≥1500 words): +0.15
  • Strong position (top 3 or page 1): +0.15
  • Search demand (>1000 volume): +0.10

3.4 Models

We train three models, all with class_weight='balanced':

  • Logistic Regression: Linear, highly interpretable coefficients.
  • Decision Tree (max_depth=8): Transparent splits, easy to inspect.
  • Random Forest (200 trees, max_depth=12): Stronger performance, feature importance available.

3.5 Validation Design

Client-holdout split. We use GroupShuffleSplit(test_size=0.25) grouped by client_id. Train: 22 clients (9,796 pages). Test: 8 clients (2,754 pages). Zero client overlap.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

Observational, not causal. We identify pages that are underperforming relative to position peers. We do not prove that editing these pages will cause CTR improvement.
  • Synthetic/starter data: The bundled dataset may have stronger correlations than real-world data. Results should be validated on the full warehouse release.
  • Engagement signal endogeneity: engagement_rate and scroll_rate are post-click metrics measured after the click.
  • Position-tier stratification reduces but does not eliminate position bias: Within "page_1" (positions 4–10), a page at position 4 still has an inherent advantage.
  • Label arbitrariness: Using the 25th/75th percentile is defensible, but other thresholds would yield different models.
  • No seasonality control: The 90-day window may capture seasonal demand shifts.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Action Playbook)

Priority 1 — Page-One Underperformers
Signal: position_tier in [top_3, page_1] AND ctr < 1.0%
Action: Rewrite title tag and meta description. Check snippet appearance in SERP.
Confidence: High.
Priority 2 — High Engagement, Low CTR
Signal: engagement_rate > 50% AND ctr < 1.0%
Action: The content satisfies users who arrive, but the snippet fails to compel clicks. Test title variants.
Confidence: Medium-High.
Priority 3 — Thin Visible Pages
Signal: word_count < 1200 AND impressions_90d ≥ 500
Action: Expand content depth. Add definitions, step-by-step guidance.
Confidence: Medium.
Priority 4 — Stale Visible Pages
Signal: days_since_last_update > 180 AND impressions_90d ≥ 500
Action: Refresh statistics, update examples, verify factual accuracy.
Confidence: Medium.
Priority 5 — Low Scroll Engagement
Signal: scroll_rate < 30% AND sessions_90d ≥ 30
Action: Improve on-page structure. Add subheadings, bullet points, visual breaks.
Confidence: Low-Medium.
